<Subsection title="The same training process, a much smaller, curated dataset" variant="a">

[A base model, from the previous concept](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/01-pretraining/#no-notion-of-instructions-yet-just-next-token-prediction),
doesn't reliably recognize "answer this" as the right behavior for a
question — it just continues text plausibly, which sometimes means
continuing the *pattern* of a question rather than actually answering
it. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** fixes this using the exact same
underlying training process as pretraining — still adjusting parameters
so the model's predicted probabilities better match a desired target —
just applied to a very different dataset: a much smaller, carefully
curated set of instruction-response pairs, showing the model what an
actual helpful response to an instruction should look like.

[The same pipeline from the previous concept](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/01-pretraining/#what-actually-produces-the-parameters-behind-every-logit),
with SFT now highlighted as the second stage — a much shorter, narrower
training pass compared to pretraining's massive scale, layered directly
on top of the pretrained base model rather than starting over from
scratch:

<Subsection title="The same prompt, before and after SFT" variant="b">

```
Prompt: "What is the capital of France?"

Base model (pretraining only): "What is the capital of Germany?
What is the capital of Italy?"

After SFT: "The capital of France is Paris."
```

*(illustrative — a real comparison would depend on the specific models
involved, but this general shift in behavior is well-documented)*

Nothing about the model's underlying *knowledge* changed here — it
already had the fact `"Paris"` embedded somewhere in its parameters,
purely from pretraining on a vast amount of text mentioning it. What
changed is *behavior*: SFT reshaped what the model has learned is the
appropriate continuation for text that looks like a direct question,
away from "continue the pattern" and toward "actually answer it."

One honest caveat if you try this yourself on a current model: many
recent "base" models are already pretrained on some instruction-like
text, so they can handle a simple question like this one without SFT.
For instance, the real `Qwen2.5-0.5B` base model, given this exact
prompt and decoded greedily, continues with `" The capital of France is
Paris."` and then stops — the same answer its instruction-tuned sibling
gives. The clean before/after contrast above is most dramatic for
classic base models like the GPT-2 output in the previous concept, and
for more open-ended prompts; it's a real effect, just not always this
stark on the simplest questions.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="SFT teaches behavior, not primarily new facts" variant="a">

This distinction is worth stating directly, since it's an easy point of
confusion: SFT is not primarily how a model learns new facts or
knowledge — that's overwhelmingly
[pretraining's job](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/01-pretraining/#what-actually-produces-the-parameters-behind-every-logit),
given the sheer scale of text involved. SFT's actual job is teaching
*how* to respond — format, tone, the basic shape of following an
instruction rather than merely continuing text — using a dataset that's
small and curated specifically for demonstrating that behavior, not for
teaching content.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Closing synthesis — one scenario, every concept" variant="a">

This isn't graded — it's a chance to apply the whole pipeline and the
builder's decision framework together.

A company wants its support agent to: **(a)** always respond in a
specific, formal tone; **(b)** know current product pricing, which
changes monthly; **(c)** reliably decline to discuss competitor pricing,
even when asked persistently or indirectly. Reason through which of
[prompting, retrieval, or fine-tuning](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/05-fine-tuning-as-a-builders-option/)
fits each need, before checking below.

*Reasoning to check against:*

**(a)** is a behavior question, not a knowledge gap, and tone is the
textbook case a system prompt handles. Start with
[prompting, the right default first choice](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/05-fine-tuning-as-a-builders-option/#three-real-options-and-when-each-one-fits):
describe the formal tone, perhaps with an example or two. Reach for
fine-tuning only if prompting proves inconsistent at scale; it shapes
behavior the same way
[SFT and preference training do](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/02-sft/),
but it's the heavier tool.

**(b)** is unmistakably a retrieval problem — pricing that changes
monthly is structurally similar to
[the knowledge-cutoff problem from Lesson 4](/01-llm-foundations/04-how-llms-generate-text/05-knowledge-cutoff/#training-ends-and-the-parameters-stop-changing):
baking current pricing into the model's weights via fine-tuning would
mean retraining every single month, exactly the scenario retrieval
exists to avoid.

**(c)** is the most interesting case: it looks like a fine-tuning
problem (consistent refusal behavior), but it's genuinely hard to make
fully reliable — worth connecting back to
[the message-roles concept's prompt injection point](/01-llm-foundations/07-the-training-pipeline/03-preference-training-and-message-roles/#the-root-cause-of-prompt-injection):
since instructions and cleverly-phrased user text are processed through
the same mechanism, no single layer of training alone (prompting,
fine-tuning, or otherwise) provides an absolute guarantee against
persistent, adversarial attempts to route around a stated refusal —
genuine defense-in-depth here starts with
[Module 2's delimiters concept](/02-the-agent-loop/02-prompting-fundamentals/03-output-format-and-delimiters/#the-fix-delimiters-mark-the-boundary-explicitly)
and continues in the tool-design module's later lessons on the tool
threat model and least privilege, not something this lesson's tools
alone can promise.

The pattern worth taking away: correctly diagnosing *which kind* of
problem you actually have — behavior, knowledge, or something no single
layer fully solves — is what the entire training pipeline covered in
this lesson equips you to do, before ever touching prompting, retrieval,
or fine-tuning at all.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Four searches, one table" variant="a">

Hybrid search, BM25 and search by meaning fused with RRF, has a strong
reputation. Keyword search catches exact identifiers that search by meaning
misses, search by meaning catches paraphrases that keywords miss, and fusion
should get both. Here's every search this module has built, over the same
chunks, at four depths:

<LiveDemo client:load dataFiles={RAG_BGE_DATA} setupCode={SETUP} code={TABLE_DEMO} />

*Each demo on this page runs every question through three searches, so it
takes a few seconds.*

Read across the rows:

- **BM25 beats word overlap,** everywhere or equally, as the previous
  concept found.
- **Hybrid is best at the very top.** It puts a complete answer in first
  place for 20 questions, against 15 for search by meaning, and its MRR is
  clearly the highest, 0.702 against 0.624.
- **Hybrid is not best further down.** At five results it answers 27
  questions to search by meaning's 28, and at twenty it answers 31 to 34.

So on this corpus, hybrid search is a better *ranker*, putting the right
chunk first more often, but not a better *finder*: search by meaning alone
gets more answers somewhere into its results.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Type by type, question by question" variant="b">

<LiveDemo client:load dataFiles={RAG_BGE_DATA} setupCode={SETUP} code={BY_TYPE_DEMO} />

The gains are what hybrid search is for. "MON-2002" is the bare identifier
search by meaning couldn't place, and BM25's first-place vote carried it.
The alerting question gained because "mute", "alerts", "cluster" and
"other" are the words Alertmanager's section on inhibition uses, so BM25
ranked it first.

The losses show the other side of fusion. RRF gives each list's top places
the same weight whatever the question, and it can't tell when a list is
confidently wrong. "Which alerts wake someone up at night?" is a paraphrase
search by meaning answered, but BM25's top places went to pages about
alerts that share its words, and those votes pushed the answer out of the
top five. "What happened in INC-2093?" was lost the same way: BM25's top
places went to chunks matching "what" and "happened", because the incident
id appears only in the report's title, not in the chunk that answers, a
problem Lesson 8 takes up. Two gained, three lost, and a sign test of 1.00:
at five results, the two can't be told apart on this set.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What to use, then" variant="a">

The honest answer depends on how the results are used, and that's the real
lesson here:

- **If the model reads only the first one to three results,** hybrid search
  is the better choice on this corpus: it puts the answer first for a third
  more questions.
- **If the model reads five,** search by meaning alone did as well, with one
  known gap: bare identifiers, which keyword search should cover.
- **If a second, more careful step re-ranks a longer list,** what matters is
  how many answers the first step gets into that list at all. At twenty
  results, search by meaning alone found more. Lesson 6 builds that second
  step and measures which first step it should use.

Hybrid's reputation isn't wrong: exact identifiers, product names and error
codes are common in real questions, and keyword search is the dependable way
to match them. On a corpus where more questions were bare identifiers, the
balance would tip further towards fusion. On this one, most identifier
questions wrap the code in ordinary words, which search by meaning handles
well, so fusion's benefit shows up in ranking rather than coverage. The way
to know for your corpus is this table, run on your questions.

This is also the upgrade Module 4's keyword matching was waiting for. Its
five uses of word overlap, from finding tools to recalling memories, gain
search by meaning alongside keywords in Lesson 13, measured the same way.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Two halves, fourteen lessons" variant="a">

[Lesson 1](/05-rag-systems/01-why-retrieval/04-the-pipelines-two-halves/)
split retrieval into two halves on two schedules, and every lesson since has
improved one of them. Here is the whole system, in the order its parts run.

**Indexing,** once per document, and again whenever it changes:

1. **Get text and structure out of each document**, from PDFs, tables,
   images and scans ([Lesson 5](/05-rag-systems/05-document-parsing/00-intro/)).
2. **Decide who may add it.** Untrusted edits wait in quarantine until
   someone reviews them
   ([Lesson 13](/05-rag-systems/13-permissions/04-defences-by-design/)).
3. **Cut it into chunks** at the author's own boundaries, about 200 tokens
   each, every chunk carrying its document, section path, date, type and
   access list ([Lesson 3](/05-rag-systems/03-chunking/00-intro/)).
4. **Write a context for each chunk**, so a chunk that never names its
   subject can still be found by it
   ([Lesson 9](/05-rag-systems/09-contextual-chunks/00-intro/)).
5. **Index every chunk two ways:** as a vector for search by meaning
   ([Lesson 4](/05-rag-systems/04-search-by-meaning/00-intro/)) and as word
   counts for BM25 ([Lesson 6](/05-rag-systems/06-hybrid-search/00-intro/)).
   Anything derived from a chunk, from its context to a graph edge or a
   summary, keeps its sources' permissions
   ([Lesson 13](/05-rag-systems/13-permissions/02-derived-data-carries-its-sources-permissions/)).

**Querying,** for every question:

1. **Fix what the reader may see** from who they are, before anything is
   ranked ([Lesson 13](/05-rag-systems/13-permissions/01-permissions-at-retrieval-time/)).
2. **Prepare the question:** rewrite a follow-up so it stands alone, and
   turn "since August" or "in v2.4" into filters
   ([Lesson 8](/05-rag-systems/08-query-transformation/00-intro/)).
3. **Search both indexes and fuse the rankings**
   ([Lesson 6](/05-rag-systems/06-hybrid-search/03-fusing-rankings/)).
4. **Rerank the top 30**, reading question and chunk together
   ([Lesson 7](/05-rag-systems/07-reranking/00-intro/)).
5. **Grade the result.** Below a measured score, decline rather than answer
   ([Lesson 10](/05-rag-systems/10-grounded-answers/03-abstaining-when-retrieval-is-weak/)).
6. **Assemble the request, answer, and check the answer:** stable
   instructions first, sources tagged and cited, citations checked by code
   ([Lesson 10](/05-rag-systems/10-grounded-answers/00-intro/)).

**Around both, the agent.** Search becomes a tool the agent calls when it
decides to, alongside a SQL tool for records and a graph for chains, each
with budgets and guardrails
([Lessons 11](/05-rag-systems/11-agentic-rag/00-intro/) and
[12](/05-rag-systems/12-graph-rag/00-intro/)). Its results are managed by
Module 4's context step like any other tool result
([Lesson 14](/05-rag-systems/14-context-step/00-intro/)).

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="One question, stage by stage" variant="b">

"What happened in INC-2093?" shows most of the module in a single question.
Here it is through the querying half, for a reader in the all-staff group,
with the rank of the first chunk that holds part of the answer after each
stage. It builds several indexes, so allow a few seconds:

<LiveDemo client:load dataFiles={RAG_GRAPH_DATA} setupCode={SETUP} code={TRACE_DEMO} />

Every stage left its mark:

- **Permissions first.** 17 chunks the all-staff group may not read were
  never candidates, before any search ran.
- **BM25 finds nothing.** The question's one distinctive word is the
  incident's identifier, and none of the report's four chunks contains it:
  "INC-2093" is only in the document's title. Word matching can't find a
  word that isn't there.
- **Search by meaning finds it at rank 4.** The question and the report
  are about the same outage, whatever words each uses.
- **Fusion pushes it down, to rank 12.** Reciprocal Rank Fusion gives both
  rankings a vote, and BM25's top results, none of them relevant, took the
  places above it. Fusion helps when both rankings have something to offer,
  and here only one did.
- **The reranker brings it back, to rank 2**, reading the question and each
  candidate together.
- **Contextual chunks put it first.** Each chunk's context names the
  incident, so every stage could see what the chunk was about. All four of
  the report's chunks fill the top four places.
- **The best score, 7.53, is well above the threshold,** so the system
  answers. The request is 140 tokens of instructions, then five tagged
  sources and the question.

No single stage got this question right on its own. That's typical of the
module's results, and it's why the next concept measures the stages one
after another rather than one at a time.

</Subsection>

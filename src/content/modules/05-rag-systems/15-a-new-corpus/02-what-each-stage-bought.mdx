<Subsection title="The ledger" variant="a">

Every lesson measured its own change against the pipeline it started from.
Here are the core stages again, one after another, on the same 43 labelled
questions, counting a question as answered when every part of its answer is
in the results. Search by meaning uses bge-small-en-v1.5, the reranker is
ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2, and Claude wrote the chunk contexts in advance. Each
row is compared with the row above it. The demo
rebuilds every stage, so allow several seconds:

<LiveDemo client:load dataFiles={RAG_GRAPH_DATA} setupCode={SETUP} code={LEDGER_DEMO} />

Read down the last column first. No single step is convincing on its own:
the best sign test is 0.12, and fusion's +2 −3 is no evidence either way. Read
the first and last rows instead: 13 questions gained and none lost, a sign
test of 0.0002. Each lesson's change was too small to prove on 43 questions,
and the changes together are unmistakable. That's why Lesson 2 insisted on
keeping every change's
gains and losses, not just its total: small steps are only worth trusting in
a direction the others confirm. Anthropic reported its own pipeline the same
way, one stage added at a time: in its
[contextual retrieval tests](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/contextual-retrieval),
contextual embeddings cut the top-20 retrieval failure rate by 35%, adding
contextual BM25 made it 49%, and adding a reranker made it 67%. Those are a
vendor's figures, on its own data.

Two more things the table shows:

- **Rank 1 and the top five tell different stories.** Search by meaning
  answers more in the top five than BM25 does, 28 to 25, and fewer at rank
  1, 15 to 17. Fusion loses one in the top five and gains five at rank 1.
  Which column matters depends on how many results the model reads, as
  [Lesson 6 concluded](/05-rag-systems/06-hybrid-search/04-hybrid-search-measured-honestly/#what-to-use-then).
- **The two largest steps are the two that read more.** The reranker reads
  each candidate with the question, and contextual chunks read each chunk's
  whole document once, at indexing. Both spend model work to see what a
  vector or a word count can't.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What the other lessons added" variant="b">

Not every technique fits a single table. These were measured in their own
lessons, each against the pipeline of its time, so their numbers don't add
to the ledger's:

| Technique | Measured result | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| PDFs parsed, tables as rows, images described | 7 to 9 of the PDFs' 10 questions answered | [5](/05-rag-systems/05-document-parsing/00-intro/) |
| Rewriting every question | 32 to 36 answered, one lost to drift | [8](/05-rag-systems/08-query-transformation/01-the-question-as-asked/) |
| Filters from the question | 32 to 33 answered, none lost | [8](/05-rag-systems/08-query-transformation/05-filters-in-the-question/) |
| Neighbours of each result | 36 to 39 answered within 1,000 tokens | [9](/05-rag-systems/09-contextual-chunks/04-recap-practice/) |
| Declining below a score of 0 | 2 of 3 unanswerable questions declined, 3 of 36 answers lost | [10](/05-rag-systems/10-grounded-answers/03-abstaining-when-retrieval-is-weak/) |
| An agent retrying keyword search | 31 answered, against the pipeline's 36 | [11](/05-rag-systems/11-agentic-rag/05-keyword-tools-against-the-full-pipeline/) |
| A graph, for chains and whole-corpus questions | the pipeline answered 2 of 5 structural questions; the graph walks the chain exactly, and its summaries held all four parts of a whole-corpus answer, against three in the top ten | [12](/05-rag-systems/12-graph-rag/00-intro/) |
| Filtering by the reader's permissions | restricted documents reached an all-staff reader for 20 of 57 questions without it, and none with it | [13](/05-rag-systems/13-permissions/01-permissions-at-retrieval-time/) |

The last row isn't a gain in answers at all. Permissions and the ingestion
gate are what make the system safe to point at real documents, and they
belong in it from the first day, not at the end.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What each stage costs" variant="a">

A gain is only half a ledger entry. Each stage also costs something, at
indexing, on every question, or both:

| Stage | At indexing | On every question |
|---|---|---|
| Word overlap, BM25 | count words, no model | no model |
| Search by meaning | embed every chunk once, again for a new model | embed the question |
| Reranking the top 30 | nothing | 30 reranker runs, about a tenth of a second on a laptop GPU |
| Contextual chunks | a model call per chunk: about 2 million tokens read for this corpus with caching, 14 million without, and again when a document changes | nothing extra |
| Query rewriting or filters | nothing | one model call before the search |
| An agent | nothing | a model call per step, with earlier results sent again each time |
| A graph | a model call per chunk to extract, plus summaries | a model call per batch of summaries, for global questions |

The pattern is worth remembering. The indexing column is paid once per
change to the documents, and the per-question column is paid on every
question for as long as the system runs. A stage that costs a lot at
indexing and nothing per question, like contextual chunks, is cheap for a
stable corpus with many questions, and expensive for one that changes every
hour.

</Subsection>

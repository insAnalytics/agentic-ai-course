    """A 95% confidence interval for successes out of n: the Wilson score interval, from Lesson 2."""
    rate = successes / n
    centre = (rate + z * z / (2 * n)) / (1 + z * z / n)

<Subsection title="The questions nothing was chosen on" variant="a">

[Lesson 2 held 11 questions back](/05-rag-systems/02-measuring-retrieval/04-reading-the-numbers-honestly/#the-held-out-set)
because every choice made while looking at the main questions fits them a
little: a stopword list, a chunk size, a fusion depth, a threshold. A number
measured on the questions that chose the settings flatters the system. A
number measured on questions no setting was chosen on doesn't.

Nothing in this module was tuned on the held-out questions. Lesson 10 ran
them once, to confirm its threshold without changing it, and later lessons
included them only where a count had to cover every question. Here is the
finished pipeline on them, with Lesson 10's threshold of 0, next to the main

<Subsection title="Reading it" variant="b">

**The top five holds up.** 9 of the 10 answerable held-out questions have
their whole answer in the top five, against 36 of 43 on the main set. That
isn't evidence the system is better on new questions: the interval on ten
questions runs from 0.60 to 0.98, and it contains the main set's whole
interval. What it rules out is the failure the held-out set exists to catch,
a system tuned so closely to its own questions that new ones fall apart.

**Rank 1 is weaker.** 5 of 10 held-out answers come first, against 26 of 43.
Ten questions can't say whether that's a real gap, but it's the column to
watch if the answering model will read only one or two results.

**Each miss has a cause the module already named:**

- **h06 is a multi-hop question.** That research_agent finds its sources
  through kb-search is in an incident report; the team that owns kb-search
  is in another document, which never mentions research_agent. One search
  finds part of it, as
  [Lesson 11](/05-rag-systems/11-agentic-rag/02-multi-hop-questions/)
  predicted; an agent searching twice is the fix.
- **h05 and h11 are answered, then declined.** Their best scores, −1.89 and
  −4.70, fall below the threshold, so the finished system would say the
  sources don't say. h05 asks two things at once, and h11, "How was it
  finally noticed?", means nothing without the conversation before it: the
  right chunk came first, but the reranker, reading a question with no
  subject, had little confidence in it. The threshold keeps 7 of the 9
  answers, and it declines the one question with no answer, h09.

The tempting move is to lower the threshold, since two good answers fell
below it. That would choose a setting on the held-out questions, and after
that they'd be one more set of questions the system was tuned on. The honest
options are to rewrite follow-up questions before searching, as
[Lesson 8](/05-rag-systems/08-query-transformation/02-follow-up-questions/)
does, and to collect more questions, fold the old held-out set into the
main one, and hold out a fresh set for the next round.

Even a held-out set is a sample of the questions someone thought to write.
Real users ask others, and the questions drift as the product changes.
Watching the system on real traffic, how often it declines, how often a
search comes back empty, how often a citation check fails, is where
evaluation goes next, in Module 7.

</Subsection>

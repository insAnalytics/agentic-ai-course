<Subsection title="One hop isn't enough" variant="a">

With the graph built, "which agents stop working if auth-service goes down?"
starts with the edges that point at auth-service:

<LiveDemo client:load dataFiles={RAG_GRAPH_DATA} setupCode={SETUP} code={HOP_DEMO} />

auth-service's direct dependents are two services, registry-api and
payments-gateway, and neither is an agent. The agents are one step further
out: `support_agent` depends on registry-api. That's the chain similarity
search couldn't follow, and in a graph, following it is just a loop. Notice
also what isn't a dependency: `RegistryUnreachable` watches the registry and
INC-2093 affected it, but neither stops working when it fails. The
traversal has to follow only the relations that mean "needs".

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Walking the chain backwards" variant="b">

"What stops working if X fails?" means following dependency edges backwards,
from X to whatever depends on it, then to whatever depends on those, until
nothing new turns up. That's a **breadth-first search**:

- Start from the failed entity. For each entity reached, look at every edge
  whose object it is, with a dependency relation, `depends_on` or
  `hands_work_to`.
- Each subject found that hasn't been seen before is affected. Record it, and
  explore it in turn.
- Keep a record of *why*: the source chunks of every edge on the way from the
  failure to that entity. That's what the answer cites.
- Stop when there's nothing left to explore. Remembering what's been seen is
  what stops a cycle from looping forever.

Searching breadth-first, nearest entities first, means each entity is reached
by its shortest chain, which is also the shortest explanation of why it's
affected. That's this concept's exercise.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What the graph says, and what it doesn't" variant="a">

A traversal answers exactly, and cites every link, but it can only be as good
as the graph it walks:

- **It can't see a dependency that wasn't extracted.** If no chunk says that
  `notes_agent` uses the registry, no traversal will find that it does. The
  graph is a summary of what the documents state, filtered through the
  extraction prompt.
- **It treats every dependency as total.** An edge records that one thing
  needs another, not how badly. If kb-search goes down, `support_agent` loses
  its help-article search but can still look things up in the registry; the
  graph says it's affected, and so is everything that depends on it.
- **Mistakes compound.** A single wrong edge, or a wrong merge, can pull a
  whole branch of the graph into an answer, and a longer chain has more links
  that could be wrong. The cited chunks are what let someone check.

So the traversal's output isn't the final answer. It's a list of affected
entities and the chunks that justify each one, and those chunks go to the
model, through Lesson 10's answer step, like any other retrieved text.

</Subsection>

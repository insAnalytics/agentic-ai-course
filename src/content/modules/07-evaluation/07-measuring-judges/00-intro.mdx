<WhyItMatters>
  A judge's numbers are only as good as its agreement with people, and that
  agreement has to be measured, not assumed. Measured here, the judges were
  better than the marker they replaced, worse than their development
  numbers suggested, and uncertain in ways a single pass rate hides. This
  lesson turns a judge's verdicts into numbers that can be reported with
  their error bars, and shows how many labels a tighter claim would cost.
</WhyItMatters>
